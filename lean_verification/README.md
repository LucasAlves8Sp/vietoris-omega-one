## Exact statement

`VietorisOmegaOne.main_theorem : VietorisOmegaOne.MainClaim`

The proposition asserts that K is second-countable, that its entire space is Lindelof, and that its entire space is not sigma-compact. There are no mathematical hypotheses on the theorem.

Here X is `OnePoint Nat`, the one-point compactification of discrete Nat. K consists of functions `Nat -> X` whose ranges are actually compact. Its topology is explicitly induced from the topology generated by the sets

    {f | range f is contained in U, and f(i) belongs to V(i) for every i in F},

where U and the finitely many V(i) are open and F is a finite set of coordinates. It is not implicitly assigned the ordinary product topology.

`orderModelHomeomorph` identifies X with the extended natural numbers with their order topology (the natural numbers followed by a greatest point). `base_topologies_equal` identifies the actual ground topology instances, and `order_model_theorem : CompactPowerClaim ℕ∞` transfers the entire compact-range construction and conclusion. This is the order-topological model of omega + 1. The formalization uses this concrete model, not mathlib's ordinal subtype representation.

## Source correspondence

Christopher Caruvana and Jared Holshouser, *An adaptation of the Vietoris topology for ordered compact sets*, arXiv:2507.17936v3, Question 1, **page 11**, with Definitions 1.8 and 1.14 and the basic-neighborhood description on pages 4-5.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17936v3

The generated family in Model.lean is the basic family in that source. The indexing cardinal is countable in this case: X itself is countably infinite and compact, and all its compact subsets are countable. Translating the source's notation and question into these definitions is a mathematical reading of the source, not a task performed by the Lean kernel. The explicit bridge to the order topology is included in the formal proof.

## Proof strategy

1. An open subset of X containing infinity has finite complement. A compact subset avoiding infinity is finite.
2. Finite or cofinite open bounds on the range, together with coordinate conditions from that same countable family, generate the topology on K. This proves second countability; mathlib then gives Lindelofness.
3. Surjections whose zeroth coordinate is infinity form a closed subset of K. On every compact family in that subset, compactness gives a finite collection of coordinates at which each chosen natural value must occur.
4. A delayed enumeration places value n beyond the bound for the nth member of a proposed compact cover. It is surjective, has compact range, and escapes that entire cover. This proves non-sigma-compactness directly, without Baire category.
